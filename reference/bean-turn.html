Four ways to turn a bean

The hub camera looks down at an angle, so turning something on the ground takes either extra drawings or some kind of 3D. Turn the dial, or let it spin, and watch how each approach copes. The ring under each bean shows where it should face (thin line) and what is actually drawn (marker).

Facing 0°toward you

Drawn views

Five hand-drawn views, mirrored to cover eight directions. The bean snaps between them in 45° steps.

Good for
Characters and anything with a face.
Watch out
Every new prop needs five drawings, and nothing exists between the steps.

Paper 3D in code

Built from simple flat shapes that the game redraws at any angle. This is how the catapult works today.

Good for
Boxy props that must turn freely, like the catapult or carts.
Watch out
Round characters come out faceted.

Pre-rendered from 3D

One 3D model, captured from eight angles into images ahead of time. It snaps just like the drawn views.

Good for
Lots of props that should all look consistent.
Watch out
Needs 3D modelling, and more angles means more images to store.

Live 3D

The same model rendered in real time with flat, toon-style shading. It turns smoothly to any angle.

Good for
Smooth turning, with lighting and shadows for free.
Watch out
The biggest change to the pipeline, and the hardest to keep looking hand-drawn.

The 3D beans are a quick approximation of the drawing, made to judge how each approach turns, not as final art.
